## The Soulmate Belief × Partner-Fit Interaction

The "soulmate" belief is a measurable psychological construct (Implicit Theory of Relationships) with a double-edged effect, entirely mediated by perceived partner fit:

- **High fit + soulmate belief** → amplified satisfaction, relationship-enhancing cognition under threat, greater longevity. (Franiuk, Cohen & Pomerantz, 2002; Franiuk, Pomerantz & Cohen, 2004)
- **Low fit + soulmate belief** → amplified conflict, reduced forgiveness, relationship-detracting cognition, and (over time) higher divorce rates. Work-it-out (growth) believers show NO fit-based bias at all. (Franiuk et al., 2004; Finkel et al., 2007)
- **Structural finding:** soulmate-model marriages report high satisfaction AND high conflict AND higher divorce; the most stable high-quality model combines an institutional base (permanency norms, community, ritual) with appreciation for the emotional dimension. (Wilcox & Dew, 2010; N=1414)
- **Limerence** (the "fated one" rush) correlates with insecure attachment and adverse childhood experiences — a trauma/attachment signal often misread as cosmic destiny. (Evans et al., 2026; N=1647)

**Application:** When a client "feels" a soulmate connection, validate the feeling but separate the marriage-event question (timing/character) from the belief-system question. The belief magnifies whatever the union actually is — so readings should encourage fit-awareness and relationship-building (work-it-out), not fate-dependence. The chart describes terrain; the soulmate is built.
