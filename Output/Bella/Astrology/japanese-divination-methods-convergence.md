# Convergence Report: How Do Japanese People Normally Do Divination?

## Direct answer
Japanese divination (uranai 占い) is not one system — it is a **spectrum across four registers**, embedded in everyday life rather than a niche occult practice:

1. **Casual / ambient** — Western zodiac ranked on morning TV; blood-type personality talk; roku yō lucky-days printed on every calendar. Low-stakes, social, disposable.
2. **Shrine & temple ritual** — omikuji (paper fortune, ~1,000-yr tradition) and koimikuji (love), tied to the tree if bad; omamori charms; enkiri/enmusubi power-spot rituals. Religious + recreational.
3. **Systematic "Eastern" calculation** — shichūsuimei (Four Pillars/BaZi Japanese school), kyūsei kigaku (Nine Star Ki), onmyōdō heritage, fūsui (directional luck); serious salon consultation for career, compatibility, decisions.
4. **Personal / direct** — tesō (palm), seimei handan (kanji-stroke name analysis), tarot; quick street reads (¥3–4k / 15 min near stations) or online.

## The load-bearing insight (consensus)
Across all four registers, Japanese engagement is **agency-preserving, not fatalistic**: fortune-telling is treated as a *prompt for reflection and reframing*, never a fixed verdict. A good result → motivation; a bad result → caution. Research shows even self-declared non-believers are behaviorally influenced, especially in uncertainty and emotional vulnerability (breakups, marriage worries) — and love/relationships dominate personal consultations (heavily among young women). Divination functions as a culturally sanctioned placebo: it organizes hope and caution and "how you accept it and move forward is up to you."

## How to position vs. Western/Vedic (for Thai client practice)
- The Japanese case is the strongest existing precedent for BooM's own rule: **mechanism over label, agency preserved, client-paced** and **love/relationship-driven**.
- Practical do's: use omikuji/koimikuji & roku yō as cultural framing in Japanese-flavored content (PAC/Instagram); borrow the "reflection prompt, not verdict" tone.
- Don'ts: do not present omikuji "#1" or blood-type popularity as measured statistics; do not merge shrine-lot luck ranks with calculational systems (shichūsuimei/kyūsei).
