## 1. RECURRING BEHAVIORAL PATTERNS & PSYCHOLOGICAL FRAMEWORKS

### A. Implicit Theories of Relationships (ITR) — the "soulmate" construct
- **Soulmate theory** = belief that finding "the right person" is what determines relationship success. **Work-it-out theory** = belief that effort builds success. (Franiuk, Cohen & Pomerantz, 2002)
- These are **two unipolar dimensions, not opposite ends** — a person can hold both. Test–retest stability r = .74. (Franiuk et al., 2002)
- **Causal finding:** Inducing a soulmate theory makes relationship satisfaction depend heavily on "is my partner ideal?"; inducing work-it-out theory produces almost no fit-based bias. (Franiuk, Pomerantz & Cohen, 2004 — 2 experiments)
- **Polarizing under threat:** soulmate theorists with a "right" partner used relationship-enhancing cognition; soulmate theorists with doubts used relationship-detracting cognition. Work-it-out theorists showed no biased processing. (Franiuk et al., 2004)
- **Longevity:** For soulmate theorists, believing one's specific partner is ideal predicted satisfaction AND longevity; for work-it-out theorists, no such moderation. (Franiuk et al., 2002)
- **Violence risk:** for poor-partner-fit couples, soulmate beliefs protected against violence early but "lost protective benefits" in longer relationships. (Franiuk et al., 2012)

### B. The "flimsy foundation" finding (institutional vs soulmate models)
- **Soulmate-model marriages: HIGH satisfaction AND HIGH conflict + HIGHER divorce.** Institutional-model marriages (permanency norms, social/religious embedding): more stable, high quality — but only when they ALSO value the expressive/emotional dimension. (Wilcox & Dew, 2010; Marriage Matters Survey, N=1414, Louisiana)
- **Nock's core claim supported:** love alone ("narrow expressive, sexual, individualistic terms") is a flimsy foundation for marriage stability; norms + networks + ritual power provide the load-bearing structure. (Nock, 1995; Wilcox & Dew, 2010)
- **The neo-traditional hybrid** (institutional base + expressive appreciation) most closely approximates the soulmate ideal *while staying stable*. (Wilcox & Dew, 2010)

### C. Limerence — the counterfeit "soulmate" rush
- **Limerence** = intense, chronic, involuntary obsessive longing for a specific person. N=1647 study: starts ~age 18, recurs ~5 times, episodes ~2 years, LO occupies ~50% of waking thought (experience-sampling). (Evans, Panton, Strawson, Floyd, Kellett & Poerio, 2026, Acta Psychologica)
- **Correlates:** adverse childhood experiences, insecure attachment, obsessive-compulsive cognitive traits, maladaptive daydreaming; 42% psychiatric multi-morbidity. (Evans et al., 2026)
- **Implication:** the "fated one" feeling is psychologically often a trauma/attachment signal being misread as cosmic destiny — a key blind-spot for astrology readers who encourage "you'll know when you feel it."

### D. Romantic beliefs & satisfaction — the replication problem
- A recent US quasi-experiment (N=135) found **romantic beliefs did NOT predict relationship satisfaction, well-being, or distress** (R² ≈ 0.01, n.s.). (Pinsky dissertation, PCOM)
- Note the tension with ITR findings: endorsement of *generic romantic beliefs* (RBI-style) is not the same as the *fit-dependent soulmate belief* that ITR research captures — indicating the "soulmate" effect is **moderated by perceived partner fit**, not a blanket belief.
- Attachment style moderates endorsement of relationship beliefs and their link to satisfaction. (Stackert & Bursik, 2003; Zagefka, 2022)

### E. Mate-selection heuristics & "recognition" feeling
- Partner-fit discrepancy (current partner vs ideal partner) predicts satisfaction **only for strong destiny/soulmate believers**. (Knee, Nanayakkara, Vietor, Neighbors & Patrick, 2001)
- Forgiveness in developing relationships is moderated by ITR × partner fit: soulmate theorists forgive when fit is high, are unforgiving when fit is doubted. (Finkel et al., 2007; study in Personality & Individual Differences)

## 2. PSYCHOLOGICAL BLIND-SPOT MARKERS

1. **Attachment insecurity is the root engine** of both limerence and the compulsive search for a "soulmate." (Evans et al., 2026; Stackert & Bursik, 2003)
2. **The "recognition" / "instant soulmate" feeling is unfalsifiable post-hoc** — the same felt-recognition is reported in relationships that later fail (Wilcox & Dew's high-conflict soulmate marriages).
3. **No psychological source supports the claim that a fated, destined partner exists.** The empirical effect is entirely about *beliefs interacting with perceived fit* — destiny is a framing device, not a discovered object.
4. **Guna matching's implicit psychology is real**: temperament (Gana), mental wavelength (Graha Maitri), sexual temperament (Yoni), constitution (Nadi) approximate genuine compatibility dimensions — but the *score* has never been validated against outcomes.
