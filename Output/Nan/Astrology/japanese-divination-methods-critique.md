# Critique: Japanese Divination Methods

## Strengths
- Strong, Japan-resident and peer-reviewed-anchored source base for a cultural topic.
- Method taxonomy is discriminating (casual vs. shrine vs. systematic-east vs. personal) and does not collapse distinct systems.
- The *psychological mechanism* is the most valuable finding: divination-as-structured-reflection / agency-preserving reframe, backed by a PMC superstition study (belief-deniers still behaviorally affected).

## Objections / required revisions
1. **Prevalence overreach risk.** "Western zodiac most familiar," "omikuji #1 for tourists," "blood type widely known" — all defensible qualitatively but must NEVER be rendered as hard statistics in synthesis. Fix: prefix with "culturally, sources widely report…" not "% of Japanese."
2. **Conflation hazard: omikuji rank vs. star system.** Omikuji is a shrine lot ritual, NOT on the same axis as shichūsuimei/kyūsei kigaku (calculational). Keep categories separate in any table — the current taxonomy already does; synthesis must not merge.
3. **Roku yō underexplained.** Import from onmyōdō hemerology is right, but its *everyday* weight (weddings/funerals) deserves one explicit line in synthesis.
4. **Psycholey: source is single-study + essays.** PMC study is one RCT-adjacent exploration; Seko "culturally sanctioned placebo" is an essayist's frame, not empirical. Label as *frameworks*, not proven psychology — critique is one step softer than the Nut/CK text implies.
5. **Bridge to Thai practice is speculative but useful.** Keep it clearly labeled as "implication for practice," not factual about Thai clients. Love-dominance claim is a reasonable transfer, not measured Thai data.
